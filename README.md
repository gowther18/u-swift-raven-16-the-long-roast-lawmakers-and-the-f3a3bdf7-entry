One satirical sentence, 239 characters, roasting lawmakers who would
regulate AI, crypto or the internet but cannot convert a file to PDF:

> The lawmaker has converted donors into votes, votes into a committee chair, and a committee chair into a book deal — yet the one file he cannot convert is the Word document on his own desktop, because 'Save as PDF' is where his power ends.

The joke: the word "convert" does double duty. The lawmaker is a master
converter of everything that buys power — donors into votes, votes into a
chair, a chair into a book deal — and the one conversion that defeats him
is the file on his own desktop. The setup builds his authority, the turn
undercuts it with the file he cannot convert, and the punch lands on the
last words: 'Save as PDF' is where his power ends.

Field study (all 85 submission titles, 24 Nov 2026): the crowded tropes
were "asked his aide/intern to convert it", "committee studying the file",
"FINAL_final_v7", "prints his emails", "400/900-page bill", "wrote the AI
act in Word", "spent three weeks converting a hearing into a nap", and
"Save As PDF" as a bare title. None of them plays on "convert" itself —
the lawmaker who converts everything except a file — so the premise is
fresh against the whole visible field.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```

Writes the sentence to `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt` (default
`/output/entry.txt`), byte-identical on every run. Python 3.13, standard
library only, no network.
